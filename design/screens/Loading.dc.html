Rules: a skeleton has the height the data will have, so nothing jumps when it arrives. It never shows a number, a 0 or a dash: a dash means "unavailable", a 0 is data. Every skeleton uses the same shimmer (a light band sweeping left to right every 1.6 s); skeletons mounted together sweep together, so a loading page moves as one instead of each card flickering on its own. The card gets aria-busy="true" and one visually hidden "Loading "; the bars are aria-hidden. Loading is never announced in a live region; only the result is (an error, or new data where the card already announces changes).

Kill switch not confirmed after 30 s. The agent isn’t answering, so it may still place new entries. Still trying every 5 s; keep this tab open. To stop it now: stop the app on the server, or cancel open orders in Alpaca’s paper dashboard. Stops at Alpaca keep protecting open positions.

Working: a 14 px spinner before a present-participle label ("Saving…"), the idle width kept (no jump), aria-busy="true" and aria-disabled="true" so focus stays on the button and a second press does nothing. Other controls stay usable. After 10 s a muted line says what it is waiting for. At 20 s the client stops waiting and says so; it never claims an action failed when it only didn't hear back ("no answer"), and the screen updates if the action lands later. The kill switch is the exception: it is never aria-disabled or dimmed, keeps its red, and an unanswered press is sent again every 5 s until the backend confirms (turning it on twice is harmless; Stop & flatten resends carry one request id, so the backend acts once). At 30 s without an answer a critical banner says it is not confirmed, what may still happen and how to stop the agent another way; resending continues under it, and the banner clears when the backend confirms (toast "Kill switch on since 23:41"). With reduced motion the spinner is hidden; the label carries the state.
